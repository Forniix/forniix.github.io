01 — PHILOSOPHY

Every setup earns its place with a spreadsheet, not a feeling.

No forward promises. Every figure on this page is a historical backtest result, shown to explain method — not to solicit trades.

Markets don't reward conviction. They reward the trader who already knows what happens across a thousand version of the same morning.

FORNIX strategies begin with a single, mechanical idea — an opening range break, a fixed stop, a fixed target — and are then run against years of price data before they're allowed anywhere near a live chart.

The work leans on ICT-derived market-structure concepts, applied to opening ranges on index futures and Indian derivatives, then stress-tested month by month, year by year, side by side.

A

Rules before results

Entry, stop, and target are fixed before the backtest runs — never adjusted afterward to flatter the equity curve.

B

Seasonality over single years

Every strategy is pooled across five-plus years to separate a genuine calendar-month edge from one lucky season.

C

Buy and sell, tested apart

Long and short variants are backtested independently, then compared head-to-head — most setups favor one side of the market.

02 — THE CORE FRAMEWORK

Four conditions have to agree before a trade counts.

Adapted from multi-time-frame momentum and wave-based trading theory — the backbone underneath the individual strategies.
Market Condition→Momentum→Pattern→Price Target→Time Target→Entry→Stop→Position Size→Manage→Exit
01

Multiple time-frame momentum

Trade only in the higher time frame's direction, and only after the lower time frame's momentum reverses to confirm it — a higher-TF bullish read that's already overbought is a warning against new longs, not an exit signal on its own.

02

Pattern recognition

Every move is read as either a trend (non-overlapping swings) or a correction (overlapping swings). An ABC correction is measured toward a Wave-C target; a five-part trend is measured toward a Wave-5 target once four legs are in place.

03

Dynamic price analysis

A reversal zone only matters where multiple Fibonacci-based projections cluster — internal retracements (38.2 / 50 / 61.8 / 78.6%), alternate price projections, and external retracements (127 / 162 / 262%) — never a single ratio traded in isolation.

04

Dynamic time analysis

Price targets are paired with a projected time window — a Time Retracement, an Alternate Time Projection, or a Time Band built from overlapping cycle ranges — so a setup isn't taken just because price arrived early or late.

Two objective entries

Trailing one-bar high/low — a buy-stop one tick above the latest completed bar (or sell-stop one tick below), trailed bar by bar until it triggers or the setup is invalidated. Smaller exposure, earlier entry, less confirmation.

Swing breakout — one tick beyond the most recent swing high or low, only once momentum, pattern, price and time already agree. Larger exposure, later entry, more confirmation.

Risk framework

Max exposure per trade3%
Max exposure, all open trades6%
Max monthly drawdown10%

Hit the monthly drawdown limit and trading stops for the rest of the month. Position size is always derived backward from entry-to-stop distance — never fixed lot sizes.

Two-unit position management

Every position that gets taken is split into a short-term unit, protected or trailed off at the first minor target, and a long-term unit, held with a wider stop while the larger trend develops and only tightened as higher-timeframe momentum shows exhaustion.

Exit logic

No single trigger closes a full position. Exits are built from price target + pattern completion + momentum reversal agreeing together, executed mechanically via the same trailing one-bar stop used on entry — never a discretionary call in the moment.

04 — THE MARKETS

Context, for anyone reading the numbers cold.

A short primer for visitors newer to derivatives and index trading.

Opening range

The high and low set in the first minutes after a session opens. It's one of the earliest structural clues a market gives about the day — a breakout above or below it is often traded as a signal that one side has taken control, though it fails often enough that risk has to be fixed in advance.

Index futures (NQ / US100)

Contracts that track the Nasdaq-100 ahead of and beyond regular trading hours. They let a strategy be tested and traded around a single number — points gained or lost — independent of any one underlying stock.

Nifty & BankNifty

India's own benchmark and banking indices, and the most liquid derivatives on the NSE. Structure here behaves differently to US indices — expiry mechanics, FII/DII flow, and local session timing all shift how an opening-range idea performs.

Profit factor & drawdown

Profit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss — above 1 means the strategy made more than it lost. Drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough dip an equity curve takes on the way there. Neither number means much without the other.

05 — PROCESS

How a strategy gets from idea to report.

Define the rule

Entry trigger, stop distance, target, and any trail logic are written down before a single backtest runs.

Run it across years, not weeks

Every trade log is rebuilt year by year — 2021 through 2025 or 2026 — so no single regime can flatter the result.

Pool the seasonality

Calendar-month performance is pooled across all years to separate a repeatable pattern from a one-off run.

Compare, don't cherry-pick

Buy is measured against sell, year against year, aggressive against seasonal-filtered — the weaker side is reported too.
